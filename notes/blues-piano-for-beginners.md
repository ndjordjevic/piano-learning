# Blues Piano for Beginners — Lesson Notes

My notes from Christian Fuchs's YouTube course, written from the auto-generated captions of all 16 videos.
Playlist: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLLSuwesi2CZL9lkXmWOpdWIqdDfrIVosq

All lessons are in C (12-bar: C7 ×4 · F7 ×2 · C7 ×2 · G7 · F7 · C7 ×2) unless noted.

---

## His teaching method (repeated in almost every video)

- **Element → combine → press into the blues form.** Learn one small element, combine it with what you already know, then play it over the full 12-bar form.
- **Count out loud**, "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and". Counting in your head lets you cheat without noticing.
- **Never lose the blues form.** Always know which bar you're in. He says students who practise alone forget the form first.
- **The left hand is the metronome.** Know which right-hand note lands on which left-hand note. He calls these "anchors".
- **Think in structures, not note names.** "Minor 3rd–major 3rd–5th", "the 9 of G", "the 7 of F7". Then the same shape moves to F7 and G7.
- **Look ahead.** While playing easy notes, your eyes are already on the next phrase. Sit back so you can see the whole keyboard.
- **Short, regular practice.** Minutes to half an hour, then stop. It settles overnight. Hands in your lap between tries, and stay relaxed.
- **Small goals, celebrate each one** (his reward is a cup of Earl Grey).
- **The same rhythm glues different notes together** (lesson 10). This is his entry point to improvising.
- **Watch out for E over F7.** Licks with E natural clash with F7's E♭ (its 7th), so change E to E♭ over F7.

## Recurring elements

| Element | What it is | Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| Blues scale | C E♭ F F♯ G B♭ C | 1 |
| "Royal thirds" | RH 3-note chord figure C → passing F inversion → C7 | 2 |
| Standard turnaround | Chromatic movement in both hands over the last 2 C bars; hidden C dim, ends on G7(♯5) | 3 |
| Sixths run | Parallel 6ths starting on E, mostly half steps | 5 |
| Standard ending | LH walks chromatically down, RH repeats C | 5 |
| Run-up | Parallel line up the white keys with C on top | 6 |
| Tension element | Thumb on E♭ + 3 on A (a tritone), resolving to E/C or G | 6 |
| Slides | Grace-note slide from a black key to the white key next to it, e.g. E♭→E | 6 |
| Major blues scale | C D E♭ E G A, a happier sound | 7 |
| Drone lick | Blues-scale notes under a repeated top C (or B♭) | 4, 9 |
| Rhythmic "dress" | One rhythm reused across different notes | 10 |
| Magic triangle | C E♭ A (root, ♭3, 6), "Oscar Peterson" sound | 11 |
| Punches | A single-note line ending on a clashing high note or double note | 15 |

## Left hands used in the course

1. **Dotted-quarter shuffle** (lessons 1–5): C–G ↔ C–A.
2. **Fifth–sixth quarter notes** (lesson 6): simple, also good for slow blues.
3. **Three-note pattern: root, minor 3rd, major 3rd** (lesson 8).
4. **"Fake walking bass"** (lesson 11): the thumb stays on the root an octave up while the other fingers walk the chord notes. It imitates a slap walking bass.
5. **Octave patterns** (lesson 12).
6. **Close-position chord inversions** (lessons 13–14, D minor).
7. **Shell voicings, root–3rd–7th** (lesson 16).

He keeps saying you can stay with your favourite easy left hand and add new ones later.

## Lesson by lesson

### 1 — Easy Introduction
- The left-hand shuffle alone, then the blues scale over it, built up one note at a time: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 notes → up to G → the full octave.
- **Fingering:** 1 on C, 3 on E♭, thumb under to F, then 2–3–4–5 (F♯ G B♭ C).
- One trip up and down the scale = a 2-bar figure. Twice = the 4 C bars. On G7/F7, split it in half.
- Mindset: don't feel stupid if it doesn't work in 10 seconds.

### 2 — The Chords
- The "royal thirds": C → passing chord (an F inversion) → C7, keeping 3 voices throughout.
- Fingering 1-2-3-4, then over to 1-2-4 → 1-3-5 on F7. On F7 and G7 he leaves out the major 3rd.
- Practise without the left hand first. Lift the fingers clearly and play with confidence.
- **Syncopation** = an accent on the "and". Count out loud.
- Variations: go up or down "the little ladder" of thirds. On G, shorten the pattern to fit 1 bar.

### 3 — Combining Chords, Blues Scale & Turnaround
- Start phrases away from the root: down the blues scale from the 4th finger, with finger crossings.
- **Triplets**: the swing feel already has triplets underneath.
- Use **finger 4 on G♭/F♯** as a habit for faster runs later.
- **Standard turnaround**: both hands move by half steps over the last 2 C bars, with a hidden C diminished, ending on G7 with a raised 5th (♯5). Learn it by heart.

### 4 — Let's Build Licks
- A lick is the "taste" of blues, like a recipe you recognise blindfolded.
- Licks are combinations of the blues scale, the thirds and triplets.
- **Over F7, change E to E♭.**
- The high-energy element: a repeated triplet figure on beat 1 of each bar, which also helps you count the bars.
- The drone lick as a phrase ending. First hand jumps between registers, so look ahead.

### 5 — Easy Licks & Turnarounds (Sixths)
- **Sixths run** from E: a half step, a whole step, then all half steps. Fingering 1–5.
- Lick: the sixths with C in between.
- **F7 lick**: start on the 9th, slide chromatically to the major 3rd, then the 7th. Use the same shape on G7. Think "the 9 of G", not "A".
- **Standard ending**: the left hand walks chromatically down while the right hand repeats C. It replaces the last 2 C bars.

### 6 — Licks & Run-ups
- New LH: fifth–sixth in quarter notes.
- **Tension element**: thumb on E♭ and finger 3 on A, resolving to chord notes.
- **Run-up**: parallel movement up the white keys with C on top.
- G7 for 2 bars (the older form, instead of G7 then F7). A chromatic descending "sewing pattern" from G's 3rd and octave lands on C.
- **Slides** start here: E♭→E with finger 3 or 2. Practise a little every day and stay relaxed. A finger crossing should be "one snap".

### 7 — The 8-Bar Blues
- **8-bar form**: C | C | F | F | C | G7 | C | G7.
- **Major blues scale**: C D E♭ E G A, which sounds happier than the minor blues scale.
- Chromatic passing notes are fine when they're not emphasised.
- The same structure (minor 3rd–major 3rd–5th) works on C and on F.
- Song: "Blue Muffins".

### 8 — Brass-Style Chord Accents
- New LH: root–minor 3rd–major 3rd.
- RH "brass section": a 6th resolving down, with finger 2 sliding from the minor 3rd to the major 3rd. The hand stays in place.
- A 2-bar pattern split across the G7 and F7 bars.
- Question-and-answer phrases with jumps.
- Change E to E♭ over F7, with fingers 5–4 on E♭–D.

### 9 — The Almighty Drone Lick
- Fingering: 1–2–1 (thumb under)–2–3. Small hands can use the thumb twice more.
- Variations:
  - 4 notes instead of 5.
  - Play it twice, then go down the blues scale.
  - A one-bar version: ×4 fills the 4 C bars.
  - The "dissolved" version: alternate with the thumb, driven by the wrist, starting on an upbeat.
- A composite lick with triplets plus a two-finger slide.
- A drone on B♭ (the 7th) instead of C.
- It uses only minor blues scale notes, so it fits all three chords.

### 10 — Combining Small Licks (the "rhythmic dress")
- Give scale notes a fixed rhythm and use a sentence to remember it: "I don't need money", "I love you no more".
- Put **the same rhythm on different elements** (major blues scale, minor blues scale, drone lick, royal thirds) and they belong together.
- He calls this the first, safe step into improvising: you can hardly play anything wrong inside the rhythm.

### 11 — Must-Have Lick, Great Left Hand
- New LH: the **fake walking bass**.
- **Magic triangle** C E♭ A, with a rhythm remembered as "I don't want to pay this, said the guest". The thumbs of both hands meet in the middle.
- Magic triangle + 3 blues-scale notes in straight 8ths.
- An upward two-finger slide. The rhythm is remembered as "Get out of here, you jerk".
- "You must be able to play the standard turnaround at any time."
- He mentions that *Easy Life Blues* uses everything from lessons 1–7.

### 12 — Easy Licks & Riffs
- A jam that stays on C, not the 12-bar form, with a gospel feel. LH in octaves.
- Short RH figures: from the ♭7 down, from E♭, slides, and a riff.
- A mini turnaround (F → G, chromatic), call and response.
- Harder left-hand options for "advanced beginners".

### 13 — St. James Infirmary (D minor)
- LH chords in close inversions: Dm, A7 (inversion, 5th left out), Gm, B♭7. Practise them separately first.
- **D minor blues scale** + a "tail" note gives a phrase.
- Drone lick with a two-finger power slide.
- **Improvising tip:** scale notes *and* chord notes are both safe.
- Think in modules. The "pain" ending is a drone lick, doubled.

### 14 — St. Louis Blues
- Learn the piece by understanding it: Dm/A7 patterns, chord tones added under the melody.
- B part: D, G, A, with the fifth–sixth LH. The melody is built from chord shapes (D triad, the top of G7).
- Every F gets a B added (the 3rd of G).
- **Fills** go in the gaps of the melody. "MMO" = minor 3rd, major 3rd, octave.
- Ending: an E♭7 at the end, or the turnaround back to the A7.

### 15 — Slow Blues (punches)
- A single-note blues-scale line ending on a **punch**: a high note played with a clashing or second note, using hand weight.
- Basic punches: double them for intensity.
- Longer 2-bar lines with a punch at the end: ×2 = 4 C bars. Split the line over the G7 and F7 bars.
- A special punch: the ♭7 and ♭5 (B♭ + F♯/G♭) together with F.
- Look ahead while playing the easy notes. Triplets throughout.

### 16 — Extended Chords & Licks
- New progression **C7 – A7 – D7 – G7** with shell voicings (root, 3rd, 7th).
- RH: magic triangle, the top of the blues scale, slow 16ths, a two-finger sliding-6th trick with E♭ on top, royal thirds going down, and "circling the E".
- **He says this is where the beginners series ends and it continues as the *Easy Blues Piano Course*.**
